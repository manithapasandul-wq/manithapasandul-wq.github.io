Propulsion Project

Propeller Design Using Blade Element Theory

Department of Mechanical Engineering · University of Moratuwa

This project involved the aerodynamic and geometric design of a two-bladed twin-propeller system for a 1,500 kg aircraft. The aircraft was required to reach a take-off speed of 220 km/h within a 1 km runway at sea-level conditions. The propulsion system used an engine speed of 6,000 rpm with a 1:2 reduction gearbox, giving a propeller rotational speed of 3,000 rpm. The total required thrust was calculated by considering aircraft acceleration, aerodynamic drag, and the assumed climb angle at take-off. The resulting thrust requirement was approximately 8.1 kN. Since the aircraft used two propellers, each propeller was required to generate about 4.05 kN of thrust.

Design, Analysis and Modelling

Propeller Size and Aerofoil Selection

The blade-tip speed was limited to Mach 0.74 to avoid excessive compressibility effects, noise, and efficiency loss. Based on the propeller rotational speed, the maximum propeller radius was calculated as approximately 0.78 m, resulting in a diameter of about 1.56 m.

Several NACA aerofoil sections were compared using their lift coefficient, drag coefficient, and suitable operating angle of attack. The NACA 6415 aerofoil was selected because it produced a high lift coefficient with comparatively low drag at the selected design condition. At an angle of attack of approximately 13°, the design used:

  • CL = 1.791
  • CD = 0.02515

This made the aerofoil suitable for generating the high blade forces required during take-off.

Blade Element Theory Analysis

The aerodynamic portion of each blade was divided into 20 radial elements. Blade Element Theory was then used to calculate the local aerodynamic behaviour of every section. For each element, the following parameters were determined:

  • Radial position
  • Local chord length
  • Rotational velocity
  • Axial and tangential flow velocities
  • Resultant relative velocity
  • Inflow angle
  • Blade-pitch angle
  • Elemental thrust
  • Elemental torque

The blade-pitch angle varied along the radius because the rotational velocity increased from the root toward the tip. A larger pitch angle was required near the root, while a smaller pitch angle was used near the tip, so the calculated twist reduced from a high value in the inner blade region toward the tip.

The chord also reduced gradually toward the blade tip. This taper helped provide adequate aerodynamic area and structural strength near the root while limiting drag and loading at the outer section.

MATLAB Iterative Routine

A MATLAB program was developed to automate the calculations for all 20 blade elements. Initial estimates were assigned for the axial and tangential induction velocities. These values were used to calculate the local flow conditions, thrust, and torque.

The induction velocities were then updated using momentum-theory relationships. This process was repeated until the difference between successive values fell below the convergence tolerance.

The first blade element converged after 12 iterations, while most other elements converged within approximately 7 to 11 iterations. The routine produced the final chord, pitch-angle, thrust, and torque distributions along the blade span.

Final Propeller Performance

The final calculation produced the following performance values for one propeller:

  • Total thrust: approximately 4,134 N
  • Total torque: approximately 1,059 Nm

For both propellers:

  • Total thrust: approximately 8,264 N
  • Total torque: approximately 2,118 Nm

The estimated total thrust was slightly higher than the calculated requirement of approximately 8.1 kN, showing that the design could satisfy the stated take-off condition.

The calculated performance parameters were:

  • Advance ratio: approximately 0.783
  • Thrust coefficient: approximately 0.228
  • Torque coefficient: approximately 0.0374
  • Power coefficient: approximately 0.235
  • Propeller efficiency: approximately 75.9%
  • Required engine power: approximately 332.7 kW

SolidWorks Modelling

The final propeller geometry was created in SolidWorks using the calculated radial chord and pitch-angle distributions. The NACA 6415 aerofoil sections were inserted at several radial locations, each scaled according to the local chord length and rotated according to the calculated pitch angle. The blade surface was then generated by lofting through these sections.

The second blade was created using a circular pattern, and both blades were connected to the central hub. The final model represented the required blade taper, twist, aerofoil shape, and two-bladed configuration.
